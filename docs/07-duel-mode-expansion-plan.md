# Duel Mode Expansion Plan

Date: 2026-05-27

## Screenshot Read

- The current vertical flow already supports a Warcraft-like campaign shell: main menu, player race carousel, enemy faction selection, and a mobile gameplay screen.
- The gameplay screen has a clear maze-building fantasy, visible wave composition, gold, HP, and a start-wave action.
- The next expansion should preserve the mobile-first layout and ornate fantasy style, but the core loop should shift from passive wave survival to an active duel economy.

## Target Experience

The MVP expansion is a single-player duel against a computer opponent. The player chooses a race, chooses or receives an enemy race, builds a maze, upgrades towers, and sends additional creeps to pressure the opponent.

The player normally sees their own battlefield. During an active battle phase, a dedicated view button lets the player switch to the opponent battlefield and watch how the computer's towers handle the sent creeps. The opponent view is read-only and must not be available during the player's build phase.

Each round contains:

- a baseline wave generated by the game;
- optional creeps purchased and sent by the player;
- optional creeps purchased and sent by the computer opponent;
- computer tower building and defense simulation on its own battlefield;
- income growth from sent creeps, paid on future rounds;
- HP pressure on both sides until one side loses.

This remains a local single-player mode. It must not introduce backend, multiplayer, WebRTC, persistence, or live balancing infrastructure yet.

## Design Pillars

- **Active economy:** sending creeps is both offense and long-term income.
- **Maze responsibility:** the player must pressure the opponent while still defending their own route.
- **Visible opponent pressure:** sent creeps should be observable on the opponent field during combat, not resolved only as abstract HP damage.
- **Race identity:** races own explicit tower and creep keys, so future content, modifiers, and backend-driven configs have a stable foundation.
- **Readable mobile controls:** tower upgrades and creep sending need compact panels with 44x44 minimum targets.
- **Typed simulation boundary:** Phaser owns runtime state; React only sends typed commands and renders event-driven UI snapshots.

## Implementation Roadmap

### 1. Typed Race Content Registry

Create explicit race, creep, tower, and modifier-ready keys before adding more gameplay.

Deliverables:

- `RaceId`, `CreepTypeId`, `TowerTypeId`, and future-safe `ModifierId` types or enums.
- Per-race registries for starter towers, recruitable creeps, display metadata, and balance references.
- Validation that every selected race has at least one tower and one sendable creep.
- Tests for registry completeness and duplicate key protection.

### 2. Duel Economy Mode

Replace the simple "wave from A to B" loop with a duel round model that can combine baseline waves and purchased sends.

Deliverables:

- Duel match state model: player, opponent, HP, gold, income, round, queues.
- Send-creep economy: cost now, income increase later.
- Baseline wave plus send queue composition.
- Deterministic computer opponent strategy for MVP.
- Separate player and opponent battlefield runtime models.
- Win/loss rules for player HP and opponent HP.
- Unit tests for income, send costs, round payout, and defeat conditions.

### 3. Opponent Battlefield View

Add a battle-only read-only view of the computer battlefield.

Rules:

- the default view is the player's battlefield;
- the opponent view button is enabled only during active battle;
- the opponent view is disabled during build phase;
- the player cannot build, upgrade, sell, or issue commands on the opponent field;
- switching views must not pause or reset either battlefield simulation.

Deliverables:

- Typed `BattlefieldView` state for player/opponent view mode.
- Typed bridge command/event for switching the visible battlefield.
- Computer battlefield rendering with its maze, towers, incoming sent creeps, leaks, and HP state.
- Deterministic MVP computer tower-building strategy.
- Mobile-safe toggle button with clear active state and accessible label.
- Integration tests for phase-gated view switching and read-only opponent interactions.

### 4. Computer Opponent Behavior

Build the computer opponent as a deterministic simplified player, not as hidden damage math.

The computer should use the same core rules as the player:

- it has gold, income, HP, race, towers, creeps, and a battlefield;
- it can build only on valid cells that preserve a path;
- it can upgrade only when it can afford the upgrade;
- it sends creeps using the same sendable creep registry and income rules;
- it never receives hidden resources unless a difficulty preset explicitly declares a handicap.

MVP behavior loop:

1. At build phase start, evaluate available gold, current maze coverage, leak history, and incoming threat.
2. Choose one of a few strategy intents: defend, upgrade, extend maze, save gold, or pressure the player.
3. Score candidate build cells by path impact, tower coverage, and distance to existing defenses.
4. Spend a bounded amount of gold on builds/upgrades so the bot does not overreact every round.
5. During battle, send creeps according to a deterministic pressure plan and current economy.

Deliverables:

- Typed `ComputerOpponentStrategy` model with difficulty and intent.
- Pure decision functions for build, upgrade, save, and send decisions.
- Candidate tower placement scoring that uses path validation.
- Upgrade scoring based on leaked creeps, coverage gaps, and affordability.
- Difficulty presets that change priorities or budgets, not hidden behavior.
- Debug snapshot explaining why the computer made each decision.
- Unit tests and deterministic simulation tests for representative rounds.

### 5. Creep Sending UI

Add a mobile panel for buying and queuing creeps without driving the game loop through React.

Deliverables:

- React panel listing sendable creeps for the selected race.
- Typed bridge command for sending a creep.
- Event-driven UI snapshot for gold, income, queue, and opponent HP.
- Incoming pressure indicator for computer sends.
- Quick access to the opponent battlefield while the sent wave is active.
- Accessibility labels and mobile viewport checks at `360x800` and `390x844`.

### 6. Tower Selection And Upgrades

Add tap-to-select tower interactions and upgrade buttons.

Deliverables:

- Phaser selection hit handling and typed selected-tower events.
- React tower action panel with upgrade and sell actions.
- Tower level balance config centralized in constants or race registries.
- Upgrade affordability and max-level rules.
- Tests for upgrade economy and combat stat application.

### 7. Dynamic Edge Entrances And Exits

Add an optional game setting that places start and end cells dynamically on map edges.

Rules:

- endpoints must be on map edges;
- endpoints must not be adjacent;
- endpoints must not be too close to each other;
- generated endpoints must produce a valid path on the initial map;
- generation must be deterministic from a seed.

Deliverables:

- Settings checkbox in game setup.
- Typed setup command passed through `shared/lib/game-bridge`.
- Endpoint generator isolated from Phaser rendering.
- Pathfinding tests across representative seeds.
- Clear start/end markers in the gameplay screen.

### 8. Duel Readability And Feedback

Make the new duel loop understandable on a small screen.

Deliverables:

- HUD fields for player HP, opponent HP, gold, income, round, and queue.
- Battle-only player/opponent view toggle state.
- Wave preview showing baseline wave plus purchased sends.
- Simple combat/event feed for sent creeps, income ticks, leaks, and opponent damage.
- Pause and speed controls that remain keyboard accessible.
- Smoke tests for start round, send creep, upgrade tower, and lose/win conditions.

## Shared Duel Rules

Both sides play by identical rules; difficulty presets change only how well the computer decides, never what it is allowed to do.

- **Equal start:** both sides open on `DuelBalanceConfig.STARTING_GOLD` (500). Difficulty does not tilt the purse.
- **Send limits per wave** (`entities/duel-match/model/sendLimits.ts`, values in `balance.ts`):
  - total: 12 creeps per side per wave (`SEND_CAP_PER_WAVE`);
  - per creep type, by tier (`SEND_UNIT_TYPE_CAP_BY_TIER`): T1 6, T2 5, T3 4, T4 3, T5 3, T6 2.
  - The send queue is cleared when the wave starts, so the limits reset every wave.
  - The computer's preset `maxSendsPerRound` may be stricter, but the shared limits are enforced on top of it.
- **Leaks steal lives** (`entities/duel-match/model/lifeSteal.ts`): when a creep one side sent leaks on the other field, the defender loses HP as usual and the sender gains 1 life per 2 lives its sends took (`LEAKED_LIVES_PER_STOLEN_LIFE`). An odd leftover is banked per side and carried to later rounds. Baseline wave leaks steal nothing. Only lives actually lost count, and a side that hits 0 HP loses even if its own sends stole lives that round. HP has no cap.

## Duel Balance

Targets: equal-strength computer duels last 20-30 rounds, a sensibly playing side has 10-14 towers by round 5, draws are rare, and no match reaches the 40-round cap (waves must eventually outgrow any board).

Measure with the batch simulator; it plays equal-difficulty duels across seeds and prints one row per matchup plus every match as `seed:rounds+winner`:

```bash
npm run sim:duel-batch                     # seeds 1-10, normal and hard, four race pairs
npm run sim:duel-batch -- --seeds 1-20 --difficulties hard --pairs orc:elf --baseline-race human
```

Why: before the pass the wave-1 baseline could already field two tier-4 creatures (1,355 health) against five towers on a 30-cell route, so most of every wave leaked and duels ended in a draw by round 4-5. The computer also kept a large reserve, which capped its opening at four to six towers. Now the opening waves are tier-1 fodder that an opening maze holds, the computer spends its purse, and compounding creep health ends matches once the board is full.

Results, seeds 1-10, baseline undead, normal and hard over undead:orc, human:elf, orc:human and elf:undead: median 25 rounds (range 6-31), towers at round 5 median 12 per side (range 9-16), draws 6%, 40-round cap 0%. Other baselines: orc 22 rounds, human 30, elf 32.

Maze planner and spending (tower-def-02k.8, tower-def-02k.9):

- The planner scores walls by route exposure (route cells within 4 cells of each block, capped at 4 per cell of route the wall adds) plus route cost, and skips walls that leave the entrance or exit in a strip at most 4 cells wide along the board edge (`maze-planning/constants.ts`). Route cost alone picked such strip walls on seeds 4 and 5: the creeps passed one face of the wall and the blocks past the endpoint only closed the strip.
- A defending computer keeps walling its maze plan; before, one early leak froze the maze for the 10-round leak window.
- Once the plan is finished the computer spends everything above a reserve of `surplusReserveIncomeShare` of one round of income (easy 1, normal 0.5, hard 0.25, nightmare 0) on upgrades, then on towers off the route. The batch table reports the late-game result as `unspent/income r16+`.

Results after that, same batch: median 27 rounds (range 21-30), towers at round 5 median 14 per side (range 12-16), draws 16%, 40-round cap 0%, unspent gold after round 15 on average 173 against 538 income. The draws are both sides dying to the same late wave on a mirror map now that neither side's maze collapses early.

Known gaps for follow-up: draws are above the 02k.7 rate; orc loses most race pairs; nightmare did not beat hard (fixed by tower-def-02k.5 below).

### Difficulty presets

Difficulty changes only how well the computer decides; stats, gold, income and lives are equal on every difficulty. Each difficulty is a set of decision parameters, kept next to the planner that reads it (all under `entities/computer-opponent/model/`):

| Decision | Where | Easy | Normal | Hard | Nightmare |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Towers per round (`maxBuildActionsPerRound`) | `buildSpendBudget.ts` | 3 (was 4) | 4 (was 6) | 8 | 10 |
| Build spend ratio / gold reserve | same | 0.5 / 0.25 | 0.8 / 0.1 | 0.95 / 0.05 | 1 / 0 |
| Maze coverage target / surplus reserve (income share) | same | 0.25 / 1 | 0.34 / 0.5 | 0.42 / 0.25 | 0.46 / 0 |
| Plan search: finalists / expansion cap | `maze-planning/constants.ts` | 1 / 30k | 2 / 35k | 3 / 40k | 6 / 60k (was 4 / 50k) |
| Sends: max share / max creeps / economy rounds / base pressure | `sendTuning.ts` | 0.2 / 3 / 5 / 0.2 | 0.7 / 9 / 0 / 0.8 (was 0.35 / 5 / 4 / 0.4, see "Normal race balance") | 0.7 / 9 / 2 / 0.8 (was 0.5 / 7 / 3 / 0.6) | 0.7 / 7 / 4 / 0.5 (was 0.7 / 9 / 2 / 0.8) |
| Defence margins: threatened / comfortable | `threat-budget/constants.ts` | 1.8 / 3.2 | 1.6 / 2.8 | 1.5 / 2.5 | 1.4 / 2.3 |
| Leak reaction delay (build phases) | `decisionMistakes.ts` | 1 (new) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Talent valuation (tower-def-bge.5) | `talent-development/constants.ts` | none (never develops) | coarse | full | full |
| Creature valuation (tower-def-716.5) | `creature-development/constants.ts` | none (never spends XP) | coarse (stats only) | full | full |

What separates the steps:

- **Easy < normal:** easy places fewer towers a round and notices a leak one build phase late, so it starts defending a round after the damage.
- **Normal < hard:** normal places four towers a round, which starves its maze once income grows; hard places eight. Both send the same way since tower-def-i55 (normal pressures from its first round, hard from its third), so build pace alone separates them: hard beats normal 86% (seeds 1-3, both maps, 96 duels).
- **Hard < nightmare:** hard rushes pressure (sends from round 3 with an 80% pressure split and up to 9 creeps, the old nightmare tuning); every creep the defender kills pays it a bounty, so early heavy sends feed the other side. Nightmare grows income for four rounds, then sends at a 50% pressure split, at most 7 creeps, and searches its maze plan harder (6 finalists, 60k expansions; one decision stays under 80k, `planBuildDecisionMazePlan.test.ts`).

Measure with `npm run sim:duel-batch -- --seeds 1-20 --matchups normal:easy,hard:normal,nightmare:hard`. Each difficulty pair plays the four default race pairs four ways (each difficulty on each race, in each seat), so race and seat imbalance cancel out; a draw counts half.

| normal vs easy | 214/102/4, 67.5% | 230/88/2, 72.2% | 23 |
| hard vs normal | 200/114/6, 63.4% | 258/62/0, 80.6% | 25 |

Known gaps: race imbalance still dominates single matchups. Playing orc, the higher difficulty wins only 13% (nightmare vs hard) to 55% (hard vs normal) of its games; playing undead it wins 83-98%. Plan search effort and the maze coverage target barely move win rates; build pace, spend reserve and send timing are the levers that do.

Same-wave tie-break (tower-def-ago): when both sides lose their last lives on the same wave, the side that leaked fewer creeps that wave wins; on equal leaks the side with more lives before the wave wins; only when both are equal is it a draw. The rule lives in the shared settlement (`resolveDuelMatchOutcome`, used by `endRound`), so the game and `sim:duel` / `sim:duel-batch` agree, and the outcome overlay names the reason. Same batch: draws 16% to 3% (13 to 2 of 80), match lengths unchanged.

### Race balance (tower-def-zs6)

Measured as hard vs hard, all six race pairs in both seats, seeds 1-10 on the classic and the seeded map, baseline undead. Seats are mirror images (each seat-swapped duel ends the other way round, checked on all 240 duels), so the 120 distinct duels decide the score; a draw counts half.

Diagnosis: what loses a duel is baseline-wave leaks, not sends. Before the pass a side leaked 13-21 baseline creeps per match but only about 2 of the creeps its opponent sent. Defence is decided by the tower roster, and races share the `single` and `splash` archetypes, so the gap came from the two archetypes each race has alone: undead frost + support, orc frost + chain, human poison + support, elf frost + poison.

- **Chain (orc only) was the weakest tower:** about 30 damage per second over two weak arcs for 85 gold, against 25 for a 50-gold single tower. The computer built six to ten and rarely upgraded them, and orc leaked the most baseline creeps (21 a match).
- **Poison (human, elf) was the strongest:** three stacks of 12 damage per second each, ignoring armor and still ticking after the creep leaves range, so about 44 damage per second for 65 gold. It is best against the armored undead baseline, which is why both poison races led.
- **Frost (undead, orc, elf) did too little damage:** the computer takes a control tower early and fills about a fifth of its towers with frost, at 14 damage per second each. Human, the only race without frost, never pays that tax.
- **Support (human, undead)** slightly beat frost for its gold in a dense maze, which kept human ahead of elf.
- Sends barely mattered. Orc creeps already have the most armour-adjusted health per send gold. The elf chimera is the elves' pressure pick, and a small cut to its health took the edge off the race whose sends leaked most often (about 3 a match against about 2).

### Normal race balance (tower-def-i55)

Measured as normal vs normal, six race pairs, seeds 1-10, classic and seeded map (120 duels, 60 per race), baseline undead, a draw counting half. Before: orc 87.5%, human 65%, undead 22%, elf 26% (classic: 97 / 63 / 20 / 20; seeded: 78 / 67 / 23 / 32).

Diagnosis, from a per-round leak profile of the same duels (`DuelSideRoundReport.sentLeaks` against `leaks`):

- Sent creeps never leak on normal, for any race: 0.0-0.3 a match, on any send preset tried, including a full pressure split. The chain tower is not what stops them; every maze does. Sends act only through the economy (income per send, kill bounties for the other side).
- What decides a normal duel is the baseline wave. With the old preset the opening send was two or three tier-1 creeps, so the opening put little gold into towers and the early mazes leaked: baseline leaks in rounds 1-12 were 0 for orc (chain holds the tier-1 waves), 5.7 for human, 8.8 for undead and 9.0 for elf. A side that leaks is "threatened", its sends shrink to the income floor, its income falls behind, and the gap compounds into the crush. On hard the eight-tower build pace keeps early leaks near zero for every race, which is why hard was balanced and normal was not.
- The send share works as a switch through the opening send: `500 x share x threatenedSendScale / 50` tier-1 creeps in the first round. Up to a share of 0.6 that is three creeps, and only undead leaks them; at 0.7 it is four, every race leaks about 2.3 of them and steals as many lives back, the kill bounties buy the second-round tower, and early baseline leaks drop to about 1 for every race. The match is then an economy race into the crush (income after round 15 about 500 against 200), which human and elf scale into better than orc.

Changes (`sendTuning.ts`, normal only; easy, hard and nightmare are untouched):

| Lever | Old | New |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Max send share of the purse | 0.35 | 0.7 |
| Max creeps a round | 5 | 9 |
| Economy rounds (income-only opening) | 4 | 0 |
| Base pressure weight | 0.4 | 0.8 |

Results, same batch, after: orc 45%, human 64%, undead 37.5%, elf 53% (classic: 25 / 83 / 37 / 55; seeded: 65 / 45 / 38 / 52). Pacing held: median 24 rounds on the classic map and 26 on the seeded map (range 20-31), 11-12 towers at round 5, draws 3-8%, no round-cap hits. Difficulty order, seeds 1-3, both maps, 96 duels each: hard beats normal 82/12/2 (86%), normal beats easy 92/2/2 (97%).

Not reached: human is above 60% and undead below 40% over both maps. Human's edge is the classic map (83%; 45% on seeded maps), where its late crush defence leaks 12 baseline creeps a match against 16-19 for the others; hard shows the same classic bias (human 65%, undead 33% on classic, seeds 1-10). No send preset moves it, because sends never leak into a late maze. It is a map and roster question for a content pass, not a send one.

Tried and rejected: a build-only opening (no sends for the first one, two or four rounds) removes the opening lottery but hands the match to orc's early maze (orc 90-100%); the per-round creep cap never binds after the opening and cannot shrink the late economy without shrinking the opening send (a cap of 2 or 3 is the old regime); the pressure split (0.5 to 1.0) and economy rounds (0 to 3) move single races by about 10 points without changing the picture; a lower share with a higher `threatenedSendScale` keeps the opening send but leaves the late economy unchanged.

How to measure it: `npm run sim:duel-batch -- --difficulties normal --pairs undead:orc,undead:human,undead:elf,orc:human,orc:elf,human:elf --seeds 1-10 --maps classic` and again with `--maps seeded`; each map takes about a minute.

The new tier-1 creatures are **baseline-only**: they are not in `sendableCreepIds`, so every race still sends four creeps (undead keeps its two tier-1 sends and leaves the abomination out). With a second tier-1 send, the computer fills its income buys with it after the first type hits the per-type cap (6), so hard sent six archers and three wisps instead of three huntresses; on the elf pairs (classic, seeds 1-10) that took elf from 40% with the wisp baseline-only to 25% with it sendable, and the wisp's stats make no difference to that choice (the planner ranks income buys by tier, not by creature).

**What the batch measures, and what it does not.** `sim:duel-batch` plays both fields against one baseline race (`--baseline-race`, default undead). The computer opponent, however, estimates the next baseline wave from the *other seat's* roster (`threat-budget/assessDefence.ts` reads `matchState.player.raceId`), which is right in the game (the baseline is the player's roster) and wrong in the simulator for every seat whose race is not the baseline race. Until this pass that error was small, because orc, human and elf stopped at tier 4; now every roster reaches tier 6 and the estimate is as heavy as the undead one. Check: with the estimate pinned to the undead roster, the old and the new rosters play seeds 1-10 on the classic map **identically** (same 60 results), so with the default baseline the new creatures change nothing but the opponent's expectations. (Fixed in "Baseline race on the match" below: the estimate now reads `DuelMatchState.baselineRaceId`.)

### Air creeps and target scopes (tower-def-xwc.4)

Fliers (unit `moveType: air`: undead gargoyle, banshee, frost wyrm, elf
chimera, and any creature a content file marks `air`) ignore the maze, as in
WispTD. Rules:

- **Air route.** Every wave carries a third route, `WaveRoutes.air`: the octile
  direct line from the entrance anchor to the exit anchor (diagonal steps while
  both axes differ, then straight), computed once at wave start with the ground
  routes (`shared/lib/pathfinding/airRoute.ts`). It ignores occupancy, so no
  tower blocks a flier; build validation stays about the ground routes. An air
  creep of any size walks it at its own speed; `remainingRouteDistance` is
  measured along it like any route.
- **Target scopes.** Towers declare `allowedTargets: ground | air | both`
  (default `both`; see docs/08 "Target scope"). Area archetypes (`splash`,
  `chain`) are ground-only, single-target archetypes (`single`, `frost`,
  `poison`) hit both. Targeting, splash blasts and chain arcs skip creeps the
  tower cannot hit.
- **Priority.** A tower shoots the creep in range closest to its exit along its
  own route; on a tie, air before ground (a flier past the maze leaks first);
  then the nearest creep (`selectTowerTarget`).
- **Wave budget.** A flier meets a fraction of the fire a walker takes through
  a maze, so it costs `WAVE_AIR_UNIT_BUDGET_MULTIPLIER` (3) times its tier
  value of the baseline wave budget. Creature stats are untouched.
- **Computer opponent.** The threat estimate splits the next wave into a ground
  lane (maze route, towers that hit ground) and an air lane (air line, towers
  that hit air); the defence margin is the weaker lane and the shortfall gold
  the sum (`threat-budget/`). Placement values a tower on both lanes with a
  dual ledger (`damage-placement/dualRouteLedger.ts`) and the scored coverage
  weighs the air line by the air share of the lane shortfall
  (`computeAirThreatShare`): a maze that holds the ground sends every coverage
  tower to the fliers. Deterministic; the defend search budgets hold.
- **Preview.** While the roster the board faces has fliers, the build overlay
  draws the air line as a faint dashed straight line.

### Air waves across races (tower-def-xwc.5)

Diagnosis, from the per-round leak profile of the standard batch (hard vs
hard, baseline undead, `DuelSideRoundReport.airLeaks`): on the classic map the
air line is about 24 cells, a flier crosses it in about four seconds, and from
the frost wyrm wave (16) on nearly every leak on every field was a flier, two
or three a round. Ground leaks were zero for every race after round 5. So a

it stretches the four seconds for every tower on the line), the support aura
(about five fewer) and poison (about four fewer). Undead (chill + aura) leaked
about 16 a match, elf (chill + poison) and orc (chill) about 20, human (aura +
poison, no chill) about 21; the gap compounds through the economy, because a
side whose own estimate says "threatened" sends only its income floor.

Two things in the computer opponent hid part of that picture and are fixed
here, since the planner has to see what the content says before content can
balance it:

- **The computer built from the archetype, not from its race entry.** Its
  towers carried the archetype's stats and no `allowedTargets`, and its
  investment candidates were priced by race but scoped and statted by
  archetype, so an authored per-race scope or stat was invisible to the
  planner and to the computer's own towers (the player's towers already used
  the authored numbers, `resolveTowerPlacementStats`). The computer now places
  every tower through `computer-opponent/model/computerTowerEntity.ts` with
  the race's `buildableTowerId`, `allowedTargets` and stats, prices and scopes
  its candidates from `getRaceTowerConfig`, and reads an upgraded tower off
  its race curve (`getTowerStatsForTowerLevel`).
- **Poison was undervalued.** The damage model counted a poison tower's ticks
  only while the creep stood in range, at half its stack cap
  (`POISON_STACK_UPTIME` 0.5). A hit's ticks run their course wherever the
  creep goes, and on the air line a flier is out of range after one or two
  hits but carries the poison for four seconds, so the model saw about 18
  damage of a 64-damage pass. With the poison races under-rating their own
  air lane, elf (the race with the most anti-air archetypes) rated its board
  lowest of all and stayed "threatened" all match. `POISON_STACK_UPTIME` is
  now 1.

archetype, and the archer price is a hair-trigger: 45 gold took human from 0%
to 60% but put 16 towers on the field by round 5, 55 gold put undead at 0%);

### AI damage types against armor (tower-def-6nf.3)

The computer opponent reads damage the way combat deals it (Decision 013):
every estimate goes through `calculateDamageAgainst` in
`entities/creep/model/damage.ts`, the same rule `resolveIncomingDamage`
applies, so the mitigation curve exists once. The model lives in
`entities/computer-opponent/model/damage-profile/`.

**Wave armor profile.** Each expected creep (baseline draw plus enemy sends,
at the health it spawns with in that wave) is tallied per damage type as the
raw damage it takes to kill it:

```txt
killDamage[physical] = hp / mitigation(physicalArmor)
killDamage[magical]  = hp / mitigation(magicalArmor)
killDamage[percent]  = 1000                      (any hp: percent ignores armor)
efficiency[type]     = sum(hp) / sum(killDamage[type])
```

`efficiency[type]` is the hit points one raw point of that type removes from
the wave. Ground and air lanes get their own profile (`IncomingThreat.
groundEfficiency` / `airEfficiency`, carried on `DefenceLaneAssessment.
damageEfficiency`). Percent efficiency is the mean maximum health over 1000,
so a percent tower is worth more the bigger the wave's creatures are.

**Defence assessment.** The route damage ledger multiplies each tower's raw
damage by the efficiency of its type: shots at the tower's damage type
(authored override, archetype otherwise), each damage-over-time effect at its
own tick type (`onHitEffects[].damageType`, magical by default; a percent
poison counts its ticks as per mille of maximum health). Ledger values are
therefore hit points of the expected wave, and the lane margin

```txt
waveDamage / incomingHealth = sum over types of damage[type] / effectiveHealth[type]
```

is the inverse of the wave's time-to-kill with every damage type working on
it at once. With alike armors this equals the old armor-adjusted margin, so
the threat tunings stand.

**Damage placement.** `planRoundInvestments` values walls, damage towers and
upgrades from that ledger, so between two otherwise equal archetypes it buys
the one whose damage goes through the wave's weaker armor (magical against an
orc roster plated against arrows, physical against warded undead). Route gain
is priced with the ledger's effective firepower per cell, and the cheapest
wall's damage in effective terms. Single-tower scoring (defending, coverage
towers) multiplies a placement's value by `computeArmorCounterFactor`: the
tower's effective damage against the wave over the same against the wave with
its two armors averaged (1 for auras, pure percent damage and alike armors).

**Sends.** `DecisionContext.enemyDamageMix` is the other duelist's towers as
shares of raw damage per type (`computeDefenceDamageMix`; even
physical/magical while the field is empty). A creep's pressure value is the
raw damage of that mix it takes to kill it per gold, so the opponent sends the
creep whose armor the defender's towers counter worst: plated creeps into an
archer field, warded ones into a caster field.

### AI talent development (tower-def-bge.5)

The computer develops towers past grade 3 through the same rules as the
player (`entities/tower/model/talents.ts`): a branch only for a race tower
type with a tower at grade 3 and no pick yet, paid with
`TowerTalentPricing.BRANCH_COST`, then grades 4-7 one at a time along that
branch. The pick is recorded in the side's `towerBranchChoices`
(`applyComputerBuildStrategy` runs `canChooseBranch` / `chooseBranch`, then
upgrades read the branch curve), so the game, `sim:duel` and the batch agree.

**Damage model of the abilities** (`damage-placement/abilityDamageModel.ts`,
tuning in `ABILITY_VALUATION`). Everything is an expectation over the
expected wave, never a roll:

| Field | Valued as |
| --- | --- |
| `critEveryNthShot` / `critMultiplier` | shot damage times `1 + (multiplier - 1) / N` |
| `extraTargets` (+ share) | up to N more creeps of those the route packs within the tower's range, at their share |
| `pierceLine` | its share on the creeps within 2 cells of the target on the route |
| `coneDegrees` (tower-def-bge.9, `deferredMechanicsValue.ts`) | `coneDegrees / 360` of the other creeps the route packs within range, at full damage |
| `groundZone` (tower-def-bge.9) | on the ground lane: the tick rate (`damage / tickMs`) on the creeps the route packs inside the zone (at most 4), halved for creeps walking through, at the zone's damage type; overlapping zones and the zone's chill are not valued |
| `corpseExplosionShare` (+ radius, chain) (tower-def-bge.9) | half its share of the shot on the other creeps the route packs within the explosion radius (at most 4): a kill's explosion is worth `share * maxHp`, and the shot's damage turns into kills at `damage / maxHp`, so the health cancels; one generation only |
| `knockbackCells`, `groundsFliersMs`, chill `groundsFliersMs` (tower-def-bge.9) | not valued (0): a conservative floor, like a stun |
| `volleyEveryMs` (+ share) | `damage * share / interval` on the creeps in range around the target (at most 6); support towers too |
| `airDamageMultiplier` / `groundDamageMultiplier` | in full on the lane whose creeps fly / walk (the ledger knows its lane) |
| `largeTargetMultiplier` / `fullHealthBonusMultiplier` | on 20% / 25% of hits |
| per-grade `chain` | the grade's chain shape when it has one, the archetype's otherwise |
| `vulnerability` | its magnitude on the tower's own damage (when the scope covers its type) and on the field's damage over the samples it keeps marked (half for a single-type scope), over what a stronger mark already adds, times the share of the wave it reaches |
| `armor_break` | the same, with the gain read off the combat mitigation curve: the wave's efficiency of that type turned back into an equivalent armor, minus the reachable stacks' points |
| chill `freezeAtStacks` / `freezeMs` | the frozen share of the time as a full stop, a mean slow up to 0.85 |
| `stun` (since tower-def-02k.11) | the share of each reload it holds the creep (`stun / reload`, at most 1) on top of the chill: `slow + (1 - slow) * share`, never a chill of its own |
| on-hit effects of splash, chain arcs, extra targets, pierce (since 02k.11) | every struck creep counted whole: damage over time on each, and the chilled / marked share of the wave is `shots * creeps struck / arrivals` |
| slow time gain (since 02k.11) | `1 / (1 - slow) - 1` capped at 2 (`MAX_SLOW_TIME_GAIN`, a 67% slow): past it the towers reaching a held creep are busy with the creeps behind it |
| aura `damagePercentBonus` (+ type) | added to the aura's attack speed bonus for neighbours whose damage type it covers |
| aura `incomePerRound` / `goldPerKill` | the gold the grade pays over 5 rounds (6 kills a round for `goldPerKill`) at the board's route value per gold (every tower's ledger value over the gold it took), added to the grade's gain (since 02k.11; it used to lower the path price, floored at 25%) |

**Branch valuation** (`talent-development/`). For each undeveloped type the
computer values both branches on its most valuable grade-3 tower, on the same
route damage ledger (ground and air lane, against the expected wave's armor
profile) that prices walls and upgrades:

```txt
gain[g]   = ledger value at grade g - ledger value at grade 3   (g = 4..7)
          + economy gold of grade g over 5 rounds * board value per gold
pathCost  = branch price + grade 4..7 prices (120 + 950)
score     = mean(gain[4..7]) / pathCost     -> picks the branch
ratio     = gain[7] / pathCost              -> ranks the pick in the round plan
```

The better branch enters `planRoundInvestments` as a `choose-branch`
candidate next to walls, damage towers and upgrades: it ranks by its whole
path's value per gold but spends only the branch price, and picking it opens
grade 4 upgrades for every grade-3 tower of the type. A grade step along a
chosen branch is worth at least its share of the rest of the path
(`(value at grade 7 - value now) * step price / gold to grade 7`), so a
branch picked for its grade 7 mechanic is climbed, not stalled at grade 4.
The upgrade cap of a defending round does not block a pick. Grades come one a
round per tower, in order, as the curve forces.

**Difficulty.** Easy never develops (`TalentValuation.NONE`). Normal values
branches coarsely: each grade's numbers only (damage, range, reload, splash)
over the tower's grade 3 mechanics, blind to what the branch adds (abilities,
effects, aura changes), so it develops later and picks on raw numbers. Hard
and nightmare value every grade in full.

**Spot check** (`npm run sim:duel -- --seed 1`, undead hard vs orc normal;
the round line now reports the top grade per side and the final boards list
grades and picks): hard develops its Frost Wyrm Nest along Permafrost,
reaching grade 4 at round 12, grade 5 at 13 and grade 7 by 19, and its Bone
Archers along Cursed Arrow (final board: one tower each at grades 4, 6 and
7); normal picks Sticky Tar and Spear Throwers late and reaches grade 4 in
the last round. Hard wins with 11 lives (9 without development). Balance is a
later task.

trimming long branch ranges (Sniper to 10.4, Vine 9.2, Cursed Arrow 8.2,
Crossbowmen 9.2, Permafrost 7.6) and capping branch splash radii at 4.2 to
shorten elf-baseline duels: neither moved a single elf-baseline duel.

median 40, 22 of 30 at the cap; before 38 and 7 of 30, and 30 without
talents in "Armor types rebalance"). The lives that decide a duel are lost to

boards reach grade 7 (by about round 27) no elf wave leaks again, so neither
range nor splash trims shorten those duels. It is a roster and wave lever
(the elf air share, `WAVE_AIR_UNIT_BUDGET_MULTIPLIER` per roster, or elf wave
health), outside a grade stat pass; the elf row itself is balanced (52%).

elf row about 100 s) and the spot-check, one at a time. The `development`
block and the `end boards` line print with every batch.

### Creature talents authoring (tower-def-716.3)

The two branches of every creature are authored from
`docs/12-unit-talents-ru.md` (the design source) in
`src/content/units/<race>.json`, with English names and descriptions. Only
the creature abilities that exist today are used (docs/08 "Creature
abilities"); nothing was balanced by simulation, a later task tunes the
numbers. Branch ids are `<unitId>_<branch>` (`undead_skeleton_bone_legion`).

**Translating the doc numbers.** The doc's base health and speed equal the
shipped ones, so grade health and speed are taken as written. The doc has one
"броня" per creature, the shipped content two armors per race profile
(docs/08 "Race armor profiles"), so armor is read as a delta over the doc
base:

- a single armor column adds its delta in full to the race's strong axis
  (magical for undead and elves, physical for orcs and humans) and half of
  it, rounded down, to the weak axis;
- separate "Физ./Маг. броня" columns add their own deltas to each axis;
- graded armor stays at or below 25 (the authored ceiling, a content test);
- grade 4 health stays within +40% of the base: where the doc goes over, all
  four grades' gains are scaled down by the same factor.

A content test asserts the two branches of every creature use different
ability fields (`creatureTalentContent.test.ts`). Doc chances become counters
(`dodgeEveryNthShot`, `secondLife`), so the duel stays deterministic.

**Bounds clamped:** Ghoul Devourer health 46-64 to 44-56, Abomination Meat
Mountain 290-390 to 284-364, Frost Wyrm Ancient Terror 340-450 to 337-434,
Siege Engine Armored Wagon 240-330 to 234-301, Druid Bear Form 190-270 to
186-244 (the +40% cap); Dryad Spell Immunity magical armor 26 / 41 to 25 at
grades 3 / 4, Hippogryph Rider physical armor 31 to 25 at grade 4 (the armor
ceiling). No ability exceeded its bound.

**Direct mappings** (the doc mechanic exists as an ability): kill reward -N
is `killGoldPenaltyToDefender` N; "every 3 sends a 4th free" is
`freeNthSend` 4 ("every 2, a 3rd" is 3); "a leak takes N lives" is
`leakLivesTaken`; "a leak gives the sender N gold" is `leakGoldToSender`; "a
kill gives the defender 0 gold" is a kill penalty equal to the reward (34);
"a leak refunds 50% of the send price" is `leakGoldToSender` 160 (half the
tier 6 price, 320); slow and stun resistance or immunity is
`effectResistance` chill / stun; "percent / splash damage xN" is
`incomingDamageMultipliers`; "the last N% of the route xM" is
`speedBoostLastRouteShare`; "below N% HP speed +M%" is
`speedBoostBelowHpShare`; the second head is `secondLife`.

**Substitutions** (revisit when the deferred mechanic lands). "Tower damage
xN" below means `incomingDamageMultipliers` single, splash and chain at N,
the stand-in for "towers near it deal less"; "misses every N" means
`dodgeEveryNthShot` N, about 1 / (share of shots lost), the stand-in for
silencing or slowing the towers that shoot it.

### AI creature development (tower-def-716.5)

The computer spends the creature experience it earns through the same rules
as the player (`entities/unit/model/talents.ts`, wrapped per side by
`recordCreatureBranchChoice` / `recordCreatureUpgrade`). The model lives in
`entities/computer-opponent/model/creature-development/`; tuning in
`CREATURE_VALUATION`.

**Defender profile** (`computeDefenceProfile`, `DecisionContext.
enemyDefenceProfile`). The player's towers (aura towers skipped) as shares of
raw damage per second: by damage type (`computeDefenceDamageMix`), by shape
(single, splash, chain from the tower's attack kind; dot from its poison and
burn ticks), the share dealt by towers that apply each effect (chill, stun,
poison, burn, armor break, vulnerability) and the chilling towers' mean slow.
An empty field reads as single-target physical/magical alike with no effects.

**Creature value** (`computeCreatureValue`): pressure per gold of one send at
a grade, an expectation, never a roll:

```txt
landed    = health x wave growth + shield x (1 + refills) + regen x exposure + secondLife share x health
rawPool   = landed x (armored health / health) / damageFactor
damageFactor = (single x m.single x (1 - 1/dodgeN) + splash x m.splash + chain x m.chain
               + dot x m.dot x poison/burn resistance) x percent multiplier
               x vulnerability and armor break, by coverage x their resistance
exposure  = 40 cells / (speed x 5 cells/s) x route-end and low-health boosts / (1 - mean slow)
mean slow = chill slow x chill coverage x 0.5 x chill resistance + stun coverage x 0.5 x stun resistance
value     = rawPool x (reference exposure / targetable exposure) x (1 + (lives - 1) x 0.25)
            / max(0.25 x price, price x (1 - 1/freeNth) - leakGold x 0.25 - killPenalty x 0.75)
```

Armor goes through `calculateDamageAgainst` (via `computeMixedEffectiveHealth`),
so plated creatures gain against archers and nothing against casters;
`startInvisibleMs` removes its seconds from the targetable exposure; a
regeneration paused by poison counts half when the field poisons.

**Branch choice** (`valueCreatureBranch`): `gain[g] = value(grade g) -
value(base)` for g = 1..4, `score = mean(gain) / path XP` (branch plus grades
1-4: 62 x tier); the better score is picked. Against four physical archers
the Grunt takes Shieldbearer (armor and dodge), against magical splash the
Militia takes Torches (splash multiplier), against frost towers the
Hippogryph takes Whirlwind (chill resistance), Rider against archers.

**Spending** (`planCreatureDevelopment`, called by `planSendCreeps` once the
sends are chosen, in every build phase, even when no gold goes to sends). The
sendable creatures rank by how many of this phase's sends (queued and
planned) they are, then a started branch before a new one, then branch score,
tier and id; each gets its best branch when it has none and then grades in
order while its tier's experience pays. Every step runs through
`chooseCreatureBranch` / `upgradeCreature`, so experience never goes negative.
The steps come out as `choose_creature_branch` / `upgrade_creature` actions
(`SendPlannerOutput.developmentActions`, listed first in the recorded
decision) and `applyComputerSendStrategy` applies them before queuing the
sends, so those sends snapshot the new grade. Send candidates are valued at
the side's current grade (`SendValuation.creatureTalents`).

**Difficulty.** Easy never spends (`CreatureValuation.NONE`). Normal values
each grade's health, speed and armors only, blind to its abilities. Hard and
nightmare value everything.

**Spot check** (`npm run sim:duel -- --seed 1`, undead hard vs orc normal;
the round line now reports `cg`, the top creature grade per side): both sides
reach creature grade 1 by round 2-4, grade 2 by rounds 13 (orc) and 18
(undead), and undead grade 3 in round 24. Hard wins with 12 lives (11
before). Balance is a later task.

### AI branch valuation (tower-def-02k.11)

After bge.6 ten branches were never picked in any batch (Plague Cloud, Wyrm
Breath, Soul Harvest, Spear Throwers, Napalm, Siege Cannon, Plague Flasks,
Treasury, Living Water, Thornbite). This pass fixes the branch valuation, not
the content.

**New batch metric.** The `development` block now prints, under each race,
one line per tower type it developed: every authored branch of the type with
its picks over the sides that picked a branch for that type (the eligible
cases), e.g. `orc_tar_pit: orc_tar_pit_quagmire 12/20 (60%), orc_tar_pit_sticky
8/20 (40%)`, so a branch the computer never takes shows as 0 of N
(`duelBatchBranchShares.ts`).

**Diagnosis** (both branches' value per gold printed on the lead tower for
seeds 1-3, all four standard pairs, through a test-only wrapper around
`selectBestBranch`; the share of the top grade's gain each mechanic made was
read by removing it from the grades):

- **Stuns were valued as a 75% chill.** A stun is a movement effect with
  magnitude 1, so the frost model capped it at 0.75 and kept it for the whole
  reach. That was most of the value of every stun branch: Grapeshot 19,000 of
  21,400 (Siege Cannon 2,400), Grasping Vine 20,200 of 31,200 (Thornbite
  8,400), Thunder Strike 11,000 of 15,700 (Thunderstorm 2,700), Heavy Spear
  8,500 of 13,800 (Spear Throwers 2,600), Mortar 4,300 of 8,100 (Napalm 1,700).
- **Splash damage over time counted on one creep.** Every strike applies the
  tower's on-hit effects (`towerStrike.ts`), so a splash poison or burn lands
  on the whole blast and an extra target takes the full poison; the model put
  it on the target only (Plague Cloud, Napalm, Plague Flasks, Spores).
- **The effect share ignored extra creeps struck.** Chills and marks reached
  `shots / arrivals` of the wave whatever a shot struck (Quagmire's extra
  targets, chain arcs, Wyrm Breath's pierce).
- **Deep slows ran away.** `1 / (1 - slow) - 1` is 5.7 for Permafrost's frozen
  0.85 against 1.2 for grade 3's 0.55, so Permafrost's slow alone was worth
  3,000-10,000 against Wyrm Breath's whole gain of 1,650-3,500.
- **Economy only lowered the price.** Gold back cut the path price (at most
  to 25%), which gives nothing to a branch whose route gain is near zero:
  Treasury scored 0.06-0.14 of Headquarters however much it paid.
- Left as they were: the 20% large-target share, percent poison stacking
  and volley packing. Once the above were fixed, the pairs that lean on them
  (Siege Cannon, Thornbite, Living Water against Moonlight) ranked in the
  A/B's order.

**Valuation changes** (rows marked "since 02k.11" in the table of "AI talent
development"): a stun is the share of each reload it holds a creep, on top of
the chill; damage over time counts on every creep a shot strikes, and the
chilled or marked share of the wave is creeps struck per second over arrivals;
the slow time gain is capped at 2 (a 67% slow, above every untalented chill,
so building and upgrades below grade 4 value frost as before); economy gold
over the 5-round horizon is added to the grade's gain at the board's route
value per gold (`computeBoardValuePerGold`), and the price is the plain path
price. Two more were tried and dropped because they changed the untalented
game: chain arcs walking along the wave (each arc reaching `bounce` jumps out;
it rated Thunderstorm right but made the orc computer buy lightning totems
over walls, 8 towers at round 5 instead of 12, orc 67% game-faithful) and a
`1 - e^-x` coverage for the effect share (frost upgrades fell, no tower at
grade 3 by round 10, human 23% game-faithful).

### AI air lane weight (tower-def-02k.12)

The 02k.11 A/B left Crossbowmen (human archer branch, `airDamageMultiplier`
1.25 to 2.2, range 10) a hair better than Archer Company in the sim and valued
at about half. Both branches, and every placement and upgrade of the round,
are valued on one dual route ledger (`buildRoundActions.ts`
`createInvestmentLedger`), so the air lane's weight there decides how much an
anti-air mechanic is worth.

**Diagnosis.** That weight was `computeAirThreatShare`: the air lane's part of
the damage both lanes still lack to the comfortable margin. Two things kept it
low through the rounds a branch is picked (16-23):

- It reads the baseline off one typical draw (`BASELINE_ESTIMATE_RANDOM_ROLL`
  0.5 picks the same unit every draw), which is far from the wave's real flier
  share: for the undead baseline it sees 0% fliers at waves 12 and 14 (the
  real share is 15-18% of hit points), 47% at wave 10 (13%); for orc and elf
  it sees 40-84% from wave 16 (real: 7-11% and 15-20%); for human 0% always
  (16-26% from wave 16). With no fliers in the draw there is no air lane at all.
- When the air lane holds and the maze is short, the weight is 0, though the
  fliers keep coming and are what the late lives are lost to.

**Weight.** The ledger now weighs the air lane by `resolveAirLaneWeight`:

```txt
s      = expected flier share of the next wave's hit points
       = (E[baseline air HP] * growth + send air HP) / (E[baseline HP] * growth + send HP)
threat = m * s / ((1 - s) + m * s)          m = WAVE_AIR_UNIT_BUDGET_MULTIPLIER (4)
weight = max(computeAirThreatShare(...), threat)
```

- `E[baseline ...]` is the exact expectation over every roll of
  `generateWaveUnits` for the match's `baselineRaceId` roster
  (`entities/wave/model/expectedWaveHealth.ts`: a walk over remaining budget
  and units picked, each draw split by the tier-bias weights of the units
  still affordable, the fallback padding counted), so a flier's budget cost of
  `m` times its tier is in it. The sends are those of the threat estimate (the
  observed queue, or the income estimate when that is larger), so queued
  fliers raise it. `IncomingThreat.expectedAirHealthShare` /
  `DefenceAssessment.expectedAirHealthShare` carry `s`.
- `threat` counts a flying hit point as `m` walking ones, the rate the wave
  budget charges a flier for meeting a fraction of the fire a walker takes.
  Expected `s` / `threat` for the undead baseline: wave 8 10% / 0.31, wave 12
  15% / 0.41, wave 18 31% / 0.64, wave 25 40% / 0.73.
- The max keeps the spend split's view where it is stronger (a held maze still
  sends every coverage tower to the fliers).

It applies to the one ledger, so damage placement, upgrades and branch
valuation weigh the air alike. The threat estimate, margins and budget split,
and the coverage scoring of single builds (`findBestScoredBuild`), are
unchanged.
